# MGNREGA Employment Delivery & Inclusion Diagnostic

## What the verified evidence says

The available official evidence supports a national/state context review for FY 2024–25, but **not a district-level diagnostic**. The Department of Rural Development’s State Performance Report lists an approved labour budget of 243.59 crore person-days and 286.06 crore “persondays of Central Liability so far.” It separately reports 119.23% of total labour budget, 50.21 average employment days per working household, and 5.78 crore households worked. The source excerpt does not provide a precise common snapshot date for every figure, and its printed labour-budget ratio cannot be reproduced directly from the two rounded person-day figures; these source values are therefore quoted as published, not reconciled or re-estimated.[1]

The same report reports women’s share at 58.1% of person-days, SC share at 18.63%, and ST share at 18.09%. These are participation shares, not population-adjusted access or equity rates. It records 40,66,631 households completing 100 days and reports that 7% of working households completed 100 days nationally. The state/UT table spans reported values from 0% to 75%; Mizoram is 75%, Kerala 38%, Himachal Pradesh 15%, and Chhattisgarh 13%. Values shown as 0% are rounded report values and should not be read as proof of literal zero households.[1]

The 100-day metric is an output measure. A low rate alone does not show that work was denied: households may have demanded fewer days, while the legal guarantee is demand-based. The report’s summary does not give a matched district-level denominator of work demanded and provided, dates of demand and offer, payment timeliness, or the expenditure breakdown needed to test delivery bottlenecks. It therefore cannot establish unmet demand, statutory non-compliance, expenditure efficiency, or district rankings.[1] [2]

## Implications and hypotheses to test

The state variation in 100-day completion and the reported national averages justify examining where employment depth differs. They do not explain why. Candidate bottlenecks—including incomplete capture of work demand, limited shelf-ready works, staff or technical capacity, attendance/measurement delays, payment processing, and seasonal or geographic constraints—are **hypotheses**, not observed findings in the accessible dataset. Validate them with district-month demand and employment counts, request-to-offer dates, work and muster records, payment order dates, social-audit findings and worker interviews before assigning responsibility.

The inclusion figures indicate that women accounted for a majority of person-days nationally in the report, while SC and ST shares were each about one-fifth. Without comparable eligible-population and demand denominators by district, these shares cannot establish equitable access. Monitor group-specific work demand, employment provided, person-days, wage receipt and reported barriers together rather than setting an unsupported parity target.

## Recommended operating response

1. **Restore an auditable district data extract.** Request or retrieve an official machine-readable export with stable district identifiers, FY and monthly period, demand and employment counts, person-days, 100-day households, women/SC/ST disaggregation, expenditure components, data-as-of date and footnotes. Publish a reproducible source-to-canonical mapping and data revision log.
2. **Triage service-delivery gaps, not headline rankings.** Once complete records are available, prioritize districts with persistently low demand fulfilment and low average person-days per working household. Validate the weakest records with local implementation and worker feedback. Keep subgroup participation visible but do not score districts on raw shares alone.
3. **Test demand-capture and work-readiness hypotheses.** Compare recorded demand with dated applications and worker accounts. Check whether the shelf of technically sanctioned works is ready before seasonal peaks; track work opening, muster issue, attendance, measurement and payment milestones.
4. **Protect inclusion and accountability.** Review access for women, SC and ST households alongside worksite facilities, job-card and demand-registration barriers, payment outcomes and social-audit grievances. Use the statutory rules applicable to the observation date.
5. **Separate schemes across the 2026 transition.** MGNREGA stood repealed from 1 July 2026 and the VB-G RAM G Act commenced with a 125-day guarantee. FY 2026–27 straddles the regimes, so report it separately or omit it from MGNREGA trend series unless the underlying records identify the regime and transition treatment.[3]

## Monitoring KPIs for a future district panel

Track monthly and FY-to-date counts of households demanding work and receiving work; the weighted demand-fulfilment proxy; request-to-offer time and the share receiving work within the applicable statutory time; person-days and person-days per working household; households completing 100 days as a share of working and demanding households; women/SC/ST shares alongside eligible-population and demand denominators; wage payment timeliness and pending liabilities; expenditure by wage, material and administration per person-day; and validation flags for missing, duplicate, revised or regime-mixed records.

These are delivery and administrative-output indicators. Household income, consumption, asset quality, resilience, empowerment and poverty outcomes need separate outcome data and a design capable of assessing attribution. Correlation between these KPIs and outcomes would not, by itself, establish causation.

## Data and implementation limitations

- The official Public Data Portal is accessible and visibly lists district as a reporting level, five selectable FYs from 2022–23 to 2026–27, multiple requested indicators and a “Download data” control. A report/export attempt during this project returned no district rows or local file. The export format, exact schema, field definitions, freshness and revisions could not be verified.[4]
- The official data.gov.in route returned 404; a discovered “District-wise MGNREGA Data at a Glance” resource URL returned “Record not found.” No usable OGD API or download could be verified.[5]
- Accordingly, no observed district dataset, district map, district ranking, demand/provision ratio, district expenditure comparison or district-level inclusion analysis is included. The local pipeline and dashboard are ready for a vetted official export. Synthetic records are used only in tests and an optional software demonstration, with an on-screen warning.
- CSV and Excel are accepted by the project importer, but the government portal’s actual export format was not verified. These are software input formats, not asserted source formats.
